A TikTok LIVE disappears from the app when it ends, so if you want a copy of your stream you have to capture it while it is happening. This guide walks through the three practical ways to do that, what each one costs you in time and equipment, and how to set up automatic recording so you never have to remember to press a button.

Before you start, one rule applies to every method: **only record LIVEs you own, manage, or have clear permission from the creator to record.** Recording someone else's stream and reposting it can break TikTok's rules and copyright law, and it is not allowed on SaveStream. See [What you may record](/help/acceptable-use) for the details.

## Method 1: Screen-record the LIVE on a phone or computer

Screen recording is the simplest manual method and needs no extra software.

**On a phone.** Both iPhone and most Android phones have a built-in screen recorder, usually in the quick-settings or Control Center panel. Open your LIVE on a second device (not the one you are streaming from), start the screen recorder, and stop it when the LIVE ends. The video lands in your photo library.

**On a computer.** Open the LIVE in a browser and use a screen-capture tool. Windows, macOS and most Linux desktops include one, and free tools add options such as choosing a window or recording system audio.

Screen recording works, but it has real limits:

- The device has to stay on, unlocked and connected for the whole LIVE. A notification, a call or a sleeping screen can ruin the recording.
- Quality depends on the screen and network of the viewing device, not on the original stream.
- Long LIVEs produce large files that can fill a phone's storage quickly.
- You have to be there to press record when the LIVE starts.

## Method 2: Record locally while you stream from a computer

If you go live from a computer with streaming software instead of the TikTok app, the software can usually save a local copy at the same time it sends the stream to TikTok. In most tools this is a separate **Start recording** button next to **Start streaming**.

This gives you the best possible quality, because you are saving the video before it is compressed for viewers. The trade-offs:

- It only works for streams you send from that computer. LIVEs started from your phone are not captured.
- The computer must keep running, and recording and streaming at the same time takes more processing power and disk space.
- Recording starts only if you remember to start it.

## Method 3: Record automatically in the cloud

The third option is to let a service watch your channel and record it for you. SaveStream checks each channel you add about every 30 seconds. When it sees that the channel is live and **Auto-record** is on, it starts recording on its own servers, so your phone and computer can be off. When the LIVE ends, the video is processed and appears in your account, ready to watch or download as an MP4.

## Step by step

Here is how to set up automatic recording for your own channel with SaveStream.

1. **Create an account.** [Sign up](/sign-up) with your email, confirm you are 18 or older, and verify your address. New accounts receive {{signup_credits}}.
2. **Add your channel.** Open **Channels**, choose **Add channel**, and enter your TikTok username or profile link. Only add channels you own, manage or have permission to record.
3. **Buy a credit pack.** Automatic recording unlocks after your first purchase. Recording is charged at {{credit_rate}}, and checking an offline channel is free.
4. **Turn on Auto-record.** Open the channel and switch **Auto-record** on. You can also keep live alerts on to get a notification when the channel goes live.
5. **Go live as usual.** When the channel goes live, recording starts in the cloud within about a minute. You don't need to keep anything open.
6. **Watch or download the recording.** When the LIVE ends, open **Recordings** to play it in the browser or choose **Download video** to save the MP4.

## Which method should you use?

| Situation | Best fit |
| --- | --- |
| A one-off LIVE and a spare device nearby | Screen recording |
| You stream from a computer and want the highest quality | Local recording in your streaming software |
| You go live often, from your phone, or at unpredictable times | Automatic cloud recording |
| You manage channels for a creator or brand with their permission | Automatic cloud recording, one channel per creator |

Many creators combine methods: they keep a local recording when they stream from their desk, and rely on automatic recording for LIVEs started from their phone.

## Tips for better recordings

- **Test before an important LIVE.** Do a short test stream and check that the recording works end to end, whichever method you use.
- **Watch your storage.** An hour of vertical HD video can be several gigabytes. Free up space on your device, or download cloud recordings to a drive with room to spare.
- **Keep a naming habit.** Name files by channel and date. SaveStream names downloads this way automatically, for example `yourchannel_2026-10-14_2106.mp4`.
- **Download what you want to keep.** Cloud recordings are stored for {{paid_retention}} on accounts that have bought credits and then deleted, so save anything you need long term.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I record a TikTok LIVE without the creator knowing?

You should only record LIVEs you own, manage, or have the creator's permission to record. SaveStream requires this for every channel you add, and recording other people's streams to repost them can break TikTok's terms and copyright law.

### Does recording a LIVE affect the stream itself?

No. Screen recording and cloud recording only watch the stream, and they don't change what your viewers see. Recording locally in streaming software uses extra computer resources, so check that your machine can handle both at once.

### How long can a recording be?

Screen and local recordings are limited by your device's storage and battery. A SaveStream cloud recording runs for up to 4 hours, or as long as your available credits cover, and you pay only for the time actually recorded.

### What format are cloud recordings?

Recordings keep TikTok's original vertical format and download as MP4 files, which play in almost every video player and editor.
