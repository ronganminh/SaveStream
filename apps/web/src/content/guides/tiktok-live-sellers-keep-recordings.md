If you sell products on TikTok LIVE, each stream is more than entertainment: it is your storefront, your product demo and your sales pitch, all in one. Once the LIVE ends, though, it is gone from the app. Keeping your own recording of every stream gives you a record of what you said and showed, material for training and marketing, and a way to learn what actually sells.

This guide covers why sellers keep recordings, how to capture every stream reliably, and how to organize the files so they are useful later. It applies to **your own shop channels**, and to channels you run for a brand or seller who has given you permission to record them.

## Why keep a recording of every sales LIVE

### A record of what you said

During a LIVE you describe products, quote prices, explain shipping and answer questions on the spot. If a customer later says they were promised something different, a recording lets you check exactly what was said and shown. It is far more reliable than memory or chat screenshots.

### Training for your team

Recordings of your best streams are the easiest way to show a new host how you present products, handle questions and keep energy up. Recordings of weaker streams are just as useful for spotting what to change.

### Learning what sells

Rewatch a stream alongside your sales data and you can see which moments led to orders: the demo that worked, the offer that landed, the question that came up again and again. Over time that tells you how to structure future LIVEs.

### Material for marketing

A product demo you already filmed live can become a short clip for your feed, your shop page or other platforms. See [how to turn your TikTok LIVE into clips](/guides/turn-tiktok-live-into-clips).

## How to capture every stream

The hard part is consistency. A recording system only helps if it catches every LIVE, including the late-night ones and the ones a teammate started.

### Manual options

- **Screen-record from a second device.** Simple, but someone has to start it every time and keep the device awake for hours.
- **Record locally in streaming software.** If you stream from a computer, enable local recording alongside streaming. This gives great quality for desk streams, but it won't catch LIVEs started from a phone.

Manual options are fine for occasional streams. For a shop that goes live several times a week, it is easy to forget once — and that is usually the stream you needed.

## Organizing your recordings

### Download and archive

Cloud recordings are kept for {{paid_retention}} on accounts that have bought credits, then deleted. Make downloading part of your weekly routine:

- Download each recording as an MP4. SaveStream names files with the channel and start time, for example `yourshop_2026-10-14_2106.mp4`.
- Store them in one place your team can reach, such as a shared drive, with one folder per channel and month.
- Keep at least two copies of anything important.

### Add context

A folder of video files is hard to search. A simple log makes it useful:

| Column           | Example                                          |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Date and channel | 2026-10-14, yourshop                             |
| Host             | The person who presented                         |
| Products shown   | Product names or SKUs                            |
| Key moments      | Timestamps of demos, offers and common questions |
| Notes            | What worked, what to change next time            |

### Decide how long to keep files

Decide in advance how long you keep recordings, and apply it consistently. Recordings can show customers' usernames and comments, so treat them as business records: store them securely, share them only with people who need them, and delete what you no longer need.

## Good practice

- **Be open with your audience** about recording if your local rules or platform terms require it.
- **Get consent from guests and co-hosts** before reusing their appearance in clips or ads.
- **Watch out for music.** Background music in a LIVE may not be cleared for use in clips on other platforms.
- **Only record your own channels**, or channels you run with the owner's permission. SaveStream requires this for every channel you add — see [What you may record](/help/acceptable-use).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I record a competitor's LIVE to study it?

No. SaveStream is for channels you own, manage, or have clear permission to record, and adding a channel confirms you have that right. Recording other sellers' streams can break TikTok's terms and copyright law.

### Will my team be able to see the recordings?

Recordings live in the SaveStream account that recorded them. To share with your team, download the MP4 files and store them in a shared drive you control.

### How much does it cost to record a three-hour sales LIVE?

Recording is charged at {{credit_rate}}, so the cost scales with the length actually recorded. Current packs and prices are on the [Pricing](/pricing) page.

### What if two of my channels are live at the same time?

After a purchase your account records {{parallel_recordings}} at once (one at a time on free minutes). If more channels are live than that, the extra ones wait and start as soon as a slot frees up, provided the LIVE is still going.
